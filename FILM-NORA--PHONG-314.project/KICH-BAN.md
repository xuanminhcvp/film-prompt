## HOOK (≈30s) — dựng thành scene `S0` (flash-forward)
[SCENE CONTEXT] Flash-forward to six months after Walter's death. Two lawyers are in Nora's apartment. Nora has just been told she is the primary beneficiary of a $340 million estate. She is in shock. The lawyers read a provision Walter dictated himself — quoting Nora's own words. [SCENE PURPOSE] Hook the viewer with the biggest question: Who left a nurse $340 million, and why did he quote her own words in his will? Then flash back to the beginning.

LOCKRIDGE: Ms. Richardson, you are the primary beneficiary of Walter Colton's estate. Approximately three hundred and forty million dollars.
NORA: …I'm sorry. Say that — no. I heard you. Three hundred — that's not — I don't —

[END STATE] The viewer knows: a dying man left $340 million to a nurse and quoted her own words in his will. Every question is open — who was he, what happened between them, why her. Flashback begins.

## SCENE 1: FIVE FORTY-FIVE

[SCENE CONTEXT] Before her shift. Nora's apartment has a curtain instead of a wall dividing the bedroom. Zoe (5 years old) wakes Nora after only 3 hours of sleep. ## SCENE 3B — THE FIRST NIGHT
Third-floor nurses' station, then Room 314 — 7:02 PM

[SCENE CONTEXT] Nora's first night shift at Maplewood in this story. Bonnie briefs her on Room 314 — a terminal patient with no family, no visitors for three weeks. Nora decides to stay off the clock and read to him. Walter hasn't spoken a word to anyone. [SCENE PURPOSE] Establish the setting, introduce the dying-alone reality of the third floor, and show Nora's instinct to stay with a patient nobody visits — against policy. The book on his nightstand is also Nora's favorite — the first connection between them.

BONNIE: You've got a new one. Room 314, third floor. Terminal — lungs. No family on file, no visitors. Going on three weeks now and nobody's walked in except for vitals.
NORA: Three weeks? Nobody at all?
BONNIE: Vitals. Linens. Out. That's the whole assignment.
NORA: That's… that's it? He's just down there by himself this whole time?
BONNIE: They all are. That's what the third floor is, Nora. You know that.
NORA: Does he talk? Has anybody heard him say anything?
BONNIE: Not once. Faces the wall. Day shift says the same thing — he doesn't move, doesn't speak, doesn't look at anybody.
NORA: …Okay.
BONNIE: Don't.
NORA: Don't what?
BONNIE: Don't do what you're about to do. I can see it on your face. The timecard flags anything past three — it's not me, it's the system. You've got twelve patients tonight.
NORA: Then I'll stay off the clock. My own time.
BONNIE: Nora —
NORA: Look, I can't fix his lungs. I know that. But I can fix the part where nobody's in the room. Nobody should have to die just… listening to a hallway.
BONNIE: …You've got twelve patients.
NORA: I know. Save me the chart.
[Nora walks toward Room 314 with the book she brought from home]
NORA: Evening, Mr. Colton. I'm Nora. I'll be your night nurse.
NORA: Can I get you anything? Water? Another blanket?
NORA: …That's alright. You don't have to talk. That's fine.
[She notices the book on his nightstand — stops]
NORA: That's a good book. That's — hold on. That's actually my favorite book in the whole world.
NORA: Mr. Colton? Did you just… you want me to read it to you?
NORA: Okay. Okay, let me pull the chair over. Give me a second.
NORA: "The town had two roads and one of them didn't go anywhere."
NORA: …That's enough for tonight. My voice is done.
NORA: I'll come back tomorrow night. We'll keep going. I promise.

[END STATE] Nora has committed to visiting Room 314 every night off the clock. Walter hasn't spoken. The book — which is also Nora's favorite — is the first connection between them.

## SCENE 5: YOU'RE READING IT WRONG
Room 314 — one lamp, curtains drawn · 11:20 PM

[SCENE CONTEXT] Nora has been reading to Walter for several nights. He hasn't spoken a single word to her yet. Tonight she starts Chapter 2. Walter speaks for the first time — to correct her reading. [SCENE PURPOSE] Walter's first words. The correction reveals he knows this book intimately (foreshadowing: he wrote it). Their dynamic is established — he's difficult, precise, and deeply connected to this text.

NORA: Evening, Mr. Colton. Same chair, if that's alright.
NORA: Chapter two tonight. You want me to do a quick recap of where we left off, or should I just go?
NORA: …Just go. Got it.
NORA: "She stood in the doorway and whispered that she wasn't afraid — "
WALTER: You're reading it wrong.
[Nora freezes — this is the first time he's spoken in weeks]
NORA: …Excuse me? Sir?
WALTER: Page twelve. The daughter doesn't whisper. She speaks at full volume. That's the whole point.
NORA: It doesn't… it doesn't say that in the text. It says "whispered."
WALTER: It doesn't have to say it. The girl is not afraid. Everyone else in that room is afraid. She's the only one who isn't. So she doesn't whisper. She says it out loud.
NORA: …Huh. I never read it that way.
WALTER: Again. Properly this time.
NORA: "She stood in the doorway and said she wasn't afraid."
WALTER: Better.
NORA: …You know that's the first thing you've said to me? In all these nights, that's the first thing.
WALTER: You were mangling my — you were mangling the book.
[Knowledge: Walter almost said "my book" — catches himself]
NORA: Can I ask you something?
WALTER: You just did.
NORA: How do you know how she's supposed to sound? If it's not written that way, how do you know?
WALTER: …I've read it before.
NORA: How many times?
WALTER: Enough.
NORA: That's not really an answer.
WALTER: It's the one you're getting tonight.
NORA: …Okay. Fair enough.
WALTER: …That's it? No follow-up? You're not going to push?
NORA: You'll tell me when you feel like it. Or you won't. Either way, I'll be in this chair.
WALTER: …Keep reading.
NORA: Same time tomorrow?
WALTER: …
NORA: You can leave your hand on the book, Mr. Colton. Nobody's taking it from you.

[END STATE] Walter has spoken for the first time. He corrected Nora's reading with intimate knowledge of the text (he almost said "my book"). Their dynamic is established: she reads, he pushes back, neither of them backs down. He hasn't agreed to tomorrow but didn't say no.

## SCENE 8: THE WHALE
Room 314 — book open on her knees, rain on the window · 12:05 AM

[SCENE CONTEXT] They've been reading together for several nights now. They reach Chapter 6 — a passage about a whale that many critics interpret as a metaphor for grief. Nora's college professor taught it that way. Walter disagrees sharply, and in doing so reveals something personal. [SCENE PURPOSE] Walter reveals the whale is lonely, not grieving — unconsciously revealing his own emotional state. Nora reads to him like someone who "does the voices" — Walter deduces she has a child. First mention of Zoe's bird question to Walter. He promises the answer is in Chapter 18.

NORA: Chapter six. Fair warning — this is the one everybody argues about.
WALTER: Everybody's an idiot.
NORA: My professor in college said the whale is grief. That's the standard reading. Every review says the same thing, every —
WALTER: Your professor was wrong. And every review is wrong.
NORA: Then what is it? If the whale isn't grief, what is it?
WALTER: The whale is asleep. Sometimes a whale is just a whale.
NORA: Then why does the whale dream about the girl? If it's just a whale, why does it dream about her?
WALTER: …
NORA: Mr. Colton?
WALTER: …Because the whale is lonely.
[Beat — Walter said more than he intended]
NORA: …
WALTER: Don't look at me like that.
NORA: Like what?
WALTER: Like you think you won something.
NORA: I did win something.
WALTER: Read.
NORA: Can I say one thing first? Just one thing.
WALTER: You will anyway.
NORA: This is the first night this room hasn't felt like a room where somebody's dying.
WALTER: What does it feel like?
NORA: Like a place where two people are reading.
WALTER: …Hm.
NORA: That was almost a laugh.
WALTER: It was gas.
NORA: Sure it was.
WALTER: You have a child.
NORA: How — how did you get that from anything?
WALTER: You read like somebody who does the voices. Only parents read like that.

WALTER: That's not bad. It's just not right.
NORA: Then what's right?
WALTER: Chapter eighteen. Keep reading and you'll get there.

[END STATE] Walter revealed the whale is lonely — a window into his own isolation. He knows Nora has a daughter. Zoe's bird question has been passed to Walter, and he's promised the answer is in Chapter 18. This plants a payoff 15 scenes away.

## SCENE 9: FOUR PAGES OF NOTEBOOK PAPER
Room 314 — Nora at the foot of the bed, not in the chair · 1:10 AM

NORA: Mr. Colton, would you… would you mind if I read you something different tonight?

NORA: Probably not. Almost definitely not.
WALTER: Read it anyway.

WALTER: …

WALTER: …Bring another one tomorrow.
NORA: …Okay.

[END STATE] Nora shared her writing for the first time. Walter — the secret author of seven novels — told her she has real talent. She doesn't know yet who's telling her this. "Those are real stories" becomes the words that change her life. He's now demanding she bring more.

## SCENE 10: THE POWER GOES OUT

She teaches Grace the adjective rule. The shoebox of stories is shown to a room full of women who write on whatever paper they can find. Walter's legacy becomes a living thing.

GRACE: Is this the writing class? The free one?
NORA: This is it. Coffee's in the back, help yourself.
GRACE: I'm not a writer. I should say that upfront.
NORA: Neither is anybody else in this room. Sit down.
GRACE: I clean rooms at a hotel. Twenty-two years.
NORA: Okay.
GRACE: I'm telling you this because I don't have a degree. I didn't go to college. I don't —
NORA: The woman sitting next to you drives a school bus. Been driving one for fifteen years. Ask her what degree she has.
GRACE: …
NORA: What's your name?
GRACE: Grace.
NORA: Grace. What's in the bag you're holding?
GRACE: Nothing.
NORA: Grace.
GRACE: …Receipt paper. From the hotel front desk. I write on the backs of them when my shift is slow. It's stupid — I know it's stupid.
NORA: …
GRACE: What?
NORA: Eleven months ago I was working nights at a nursing home. I made nine dollars less an hour than I should have. And I wrote stories for my daughter on notebook paper because it was forty-nine cents a pack.
GRACE: …And now you own this building.
NORA: Now I own this building.
GRACE: How? How does that even happen?
NORA: I'll tell you the whole story at the end of the session. Right now — look in this shoebox.
GRACE: What is it?
NORA: Thirty-six stories. Handwritten. Every single one for my daughter. Notebook paper, crayon stains, coffee rings.
GRACE: …
NORA: This right here — this is the first one I ever showed to another adult. My hands shook so bad I almost dropped it on the floor.
GRACE: What did he say?
NORA: He said: those are not bedtime stories. Those are real stories.
GRACE: …
NORA: That was the first time in my life anybody told me my writing was worth something. I was twenty-nine years old. Twenty-nine.
GRACE: …
NORA: So I'm telling you now, Grace. Your writing is worth something. Whatever's on the back of that receipt paper — it's worth something. Now start.
GRACE: I don't know how to start.
NORA: One page. Handwritten. Bring it back next week.
GRACE: That's all?
NORA: That's all. And Grace — no adjectives. If something is beautiful, you don't have to say so. The reader will see it.
GRACE: …Who taught you that?
NORA: A very rude old man who was right about almost everything.

[END STATE] The cycle completes. Nora teaches Grace the way Walter taught her — one page, no adjectives, your writing is worth something. The shoebox is open. Receipt paper is the new notebook paper. Walter's voice lives through Nora's teaching.

## SCENE 37: THE ROOM 314 PROJECT
Third-floor nurses' station, Maplewood — same counter as the Hook · 11:00 PM

[SCENE CONTEXT] Nora returns to Maplewood — not as a nurse, but as a donor. She brings 260 books, all from Walter's collection. She meets Callie, the new night nurse on the third floor, who has the same twelve patients and the same time pressure Nora had. Nora is now wearing a coat, not scrubs. [SCENE PURPOSE] Nora passes the lesson forward to the next nurse. "One chapter, one patient, one time this week." Callie's pushback — "That's easy to say in a coat like that" — is fair, and Nora acknowledges it. The books are from Walter. Room 314 is where it starts.

CALLIE: Ma'am, visiting hours ended at eight.
NORA: I'm not visiting. I'm dropping off.
CALLIE: …That's a lot of books.
NORA: Two hundred in these boxes. Sixty more in the van outside.
CALLIE: For who?
NORA: For the patients. Especially the ones nobody visits.
CALLIE: …
NORA: Third floor especially.
CALLIE: That's kind. It really is. But I've got twelve patients tonight, and I don't have time to sit and read to anybody. I'm not being cold — I get thirty minutes for breaks and most nights I take fifteen.
NORA: I know exactly how much time you get.
CALLIE: …How would you know that?
NORA: Because I sat in that chair. The one by the station — with the wobble in the left caster. I worked this floor.
CALLIE: …You worked here?
NORA: Seven to three. Third floor. Twelve patients. Same assignment you've got right now.
CALLIE: Why'd you leave?
NORA: Long story. You don't have time for it tonight. You're Callie, right?
CALLIE: Callie, yeah.
NORA: Callie. Sit down with me for one second.
CALLIE: …
NORA: Not for an hour. One chapter, one patient, one time this week. That's all I'm asking.
CALLIE: And when the timecard flags me?
NORA: It will. Mine flagged me twice.
CALLIE: That's easy to say when you're wearing a coat like that.
[Beat — Nora doesn't flinch]
NORA: …
CALLIE: I'm sorry. That was —
NORA: No. That was fair. You're right.
CALLIE: …
NORA: Eleven months ago I owed my neighbor forty dollars for babysitting. I couldn't pay it. And I stayed anyway — not because I could afford to lose the job.
CALLIE: Then why?
NORA: Because there was a man in 314 who pushed a book across a nightstand. And he couldn't ask out loud for somebody to sit with him. He just pushed the book. That's the whole reason.
CALLIE: …What happened to him?
NORA: He died on a Thursday. Three chapters short of the ending.
CALLIE: …I'm sorry.
NORA: Every book in that van is from him. Room 314. Start there.

[END STATE] Nora has brought Walter's books back to Maplewood. She's passed the torch to Callie — the next nurse on the third floor. "One chapter, one patient, one time this week." The Room 314 Project begins.

## SCENE 38: THE PACKAGE
Brownstone office — shelves full now, the desk with the mug ring · 2:00 PM

[SCENE CONTEXT] A package arrives with no return address. Inside is a hardcover children's book: "The Girl Who Woke the Whale" — Nora's four-page notebook story, published by Walter's estate with professional illustrations. The frontispiece is not a watercolor — it's Zoe's original crayon drawing of the purple whale with the girl on his back. Walter got the drawing out of the building before housekeeping threw it away. [SCENE PURPOSE] The final gift. Walter arranged the book's publication while dying. He saved Zoe's drawing — the one Nora thought was thrown away in Scene 27. The dedication: "For Nora Richardson, who reminded a sleeping whale that stories are never finished — they are only passed on." The drawing was never gone.

DELIA: Sugar, there's a box here with no return address. You want me to open it?
NORA: …It's a book.
DELIA: Children's book. Hardcover. Beautiful cover — look at this.

DELIA: "The Girl Who Woke the Whale."
[Nora stops breathing for a moment]
NORA: …

NORA: That's mine. Miss Delia, that's mine. That's four pages of notebook paper. That's the story I read to him with my hands shaking.

## SCENE 39: ONE YEAR
Cemetery — same slope as Scene 31, oaks budding · 10:00 AM

[SCENE CONTEXT] One year after Walter's death. Nora and Zoe return to his grave. Someone has added a small metal plaque under his name: "He wrote in silence so others could read out loud." Nobody will say who put it there — Lockridge denies it badly. Nora reads the last page of "The Girl Who Woke the Whale" at his grave. Zoe does something with her hand — pressing it flat against the stone — that she learned from watching Nora. [SCENE PURPOSE] The final scene. The plaque, the book, Zoe's hand on the stone. "A big sad?" / "No, baby. Not this time. This one's the other kind." The story ends where it began — someone reading out loud to someone who can hear.

ZOE: Mama, somebody added a part.
NORA: Where, baby?
ZOE: Under his name. A little metal thing. It wasn't here last month.
NORA: …Read it to me.
ZOE: "He wrote in silence so others could read out loud."
NORA: …
ZOE: Did you do that?
NORA: No.
ZOE: Did the suit men?

[END STATE] One year later. The plaque is placed by someone unknown — probably Callie or Bonnie. The book is read at his grave. Zoe's hand on the stone. The reading continues.

## CTA
NARRATOR: Walter Colton spent fifty years writing for millions and died in a room where no one came. A nurse with unpaid rent sat down anyway — and the kindness she gave away came back to her through her own daughter's voice. Being present for someone the world forgot is not charity. It is love, and it outlives everyone.
