## HOOK (30 SECONDS)

[SCENE CONTEXT] Flash forward to the peak of scene nine. Thursday, 1 April, peak lunch service in the Larkspur Room. Eleanor Whitfield has not eaten since Tuesday and her hands are too unsteady to hold anything. Her paid aide has left the building. Vivian Ashcroft has already banned staff from sitting with guests and is standing close enough to enforce it. Knowledge state: the audience knows nothing about anyone yet. They learn the entire situation from four lines of dialogue.
[SCENE PURPOSE] Thirty seconds at maximum pressure. Show a woman choosing to lose her job in real time, and end on a promise the story then has to keep.
ELEANOR: Cold. It is cold again.
FAITH: I know. Nobody has warmed it since twelve o'clock.
ELEANOR: Hungry. I am. So hungry.

ELEANOR: Stay. Stay with me.
FAITH: I am staying. I will be here Thursday.
[END STATE] The audience has seen the act, the warning and the cost inside a minute, and has been handed a promise about a Thursday. The story now rewinds four weeks to the morning that made this woman.

FAITH: I am. It opens a week from Thursday.
VIVIAN: Well. Then you have your answer about me, do you not. I am the last person on earth who belongs in that room.
FAITH: There are twenty-two chairs, ma'am.
VIVIAN: I fired you in front of a dining room. In front of the whole floor.
FAITH: Mm-hmm. You did.
VIVIAN: And now you are standing in a loading dock inviting me to a class.
FAITH: It is a chair, ma'am. Nobody has to approve a chair.
[The entire moral of the training programme, said to the one person who used to approve chairs for a living.]
VIVIAN: That is a very strange thing to say to me right now.
FAITH: Well. It is the only thing I am teaching in there.
VIVIAN: I will. Hmm. I will think about it.
FAITH: Back row is fine. Nobody ever looks at the back row.
VIVIAN: Miss Dawson. Why are you here on a Thursday?
FAITH: I was invited.
[END STATE] Vivian has been invited to the class and has said she will think about it. Faith is in the building on a Thursday because she has been invited by the owner, which neither woman explains further.

**PART FOUR — SHE WAS PAID BACK**

## SCENE TWENTY-SEVEN: THE LARKSPUR ROOM, CLOSED FOR LUNCH

[SCENE CONTEXT] The following month, a Thursday. The Larkspur Room is closed for lunch on the owner's instruction. Julian has prepared three things and one person. Roz is serving. Eleanor has been eating properly for weeks and has regained some strength in her left hand. Knowledge state: Faith believes she has been called in for a job offer. She does not know her mother's table has been reserved permanently or that Eleanor is behind the kitchen door.
[SCENE PURPOSE] The title payoff. Reverse every specific loss with the same specific object, and hold the tears back through three material gifts so that the fourth one lands.
FAITH: Hmm. The lights are on but the door says closed. Sir, is the door wrong?
JULIAN: The door is right. Come in. I closed the room for the afternoon.
FAITH: You closed the. Sir, that is a hundred and forty covers on a Thursday.
JULIAN: Sixty-two covers. I checked before I did it.
FAITH: Okay. So why am I standing in an empty restaurant?
JULIAN: Because today you are a guest.
FAITH: Oh, no. No, I am not dressed for this, look at me.
JULIAN: You are dressed exactly right. Which table do you think?
FAITH: Mm. Nine.
JULIAN: Nine. This way.
FAITH: Sir, I can pull out my own chair, I have done it a few thousand times.
JULIAN: Not today. Today I am pulling the chair. Sit down, please. Both feet under the table.
[Spatial reversal of scene nine. The woman fired for pulling out a chair is seated by the owner at the same table.]
FAITH: Ha. This feels so wrong. My whole body is telling me to go check the pass.
JULIAN: You were fired for pulling out a chair. So this is the only correct place to start.
ROZ: Water for the guest.
FAITH: Roz. Do not you dare serve me. Roz.
ROZ: Hah. Thirty years I have wanted to serve somebody I actually like. Sit still.
ROZ: And bread. The good rolls. The ones your girl calls cloud bread.
FAITH: Roz. You planned this. You planned the whole thing.
ROZ: Mm. He planned it. I baked it.
JULIAN: Miss Dawson. There are three things on that table. Open the envelope first.
FAITH: Okay. It is a school letter. Nursing program, fall enrollment.
JULIAN: You left with three semesters finished.
FAITH: How do you know that? That was four years ago.
JULIAN: It is in your file. Tuition and books are paid through graduation.
FAITH: Sir. No. No, that is too much, I cannot.
[Beat one of four. She refuses the tuition and does not cry.]
JULIAN: It is nine thousand four hundred a month less than I was paying an agency to not feed my mother.
FAITH: Do not make it a joke.
JULIAN: I am not joking. That is just the arithmetic of it.
FAITH: Okay. What is the second thing?
JULIAN: A job. Not this one. This room becomes a training floor on Thursdays, for every aide my company hires. All of them.
FAITH: Well. Somebody has to build that.
JULIAN: You are going to build it, and then you are going to run it.
FAITH: Sir, I bent a spoon. That is my entire qualification, a spoon and a pair of pliers.
JULIAN: You did the one thing that nobody else in this building did.
FAITH: And the third thing?
JULIAN: Look at the little sign on the table. Turn it over.
FAITH: Reserved. Oh. Nobody goes hungry at this table.
[Thesis line becomes a physical object. Table nine is permanently off the book from today.]
JULIAN: That table comes off the book. Permanently.
FAITH: Sir.
JULIAN: Hmm. You are not crying. I will be honest, I expected crying.
[Knowledge: Julian has misread her completely. Money is not what breaks her, and he is about to find out what does.]
FAITH: Ha. I do not cry about money, sir.
JULIAN: Okay. Then let me stop talking about money. Hold the kitchen door, Roz.
JULIAN: Slowly, Mother. Slowly, mind that chair leg.
FAITH: Oh. Ma'am.
ELEANOR: You. Sit. Down.
FAITH: I am sitting. Look at me, ma'am, I am sitting down.
ELEANOR: Bowl. Spoon.
JULIAN: It is right here, Mother. In your left hand, and I will hold the bowl.
FAITH: Ma'am. Ma'am, what are you doing?
ELEANOR: Open.
FAITH: Oh, no. No, ma'am, I am fine. I ate this morning.
ELEANOR: Liar.
JULIAN: Hah. She is right. Roz says you never eat before a shift.
FAITH: Roz talks too much.
ELEANOR: Open.
FAITH: Okay. Okay. All right, ma'am.
ELEANOR: Small bites. Then rest.
[Peak. The phrase Faith invented for her daughter and gave to Eleanor comes back to her from the woman she fed. This is the line that breaks her.]
FAITH: You remembered that.
ELEANOR: Every. Thursday.
FAITH: I am sorry. Give me one second, I just. Give me one second.
JULIAN: Take the whole afternoon. The room is closed.
FAITH: It is very warm in here, that is all it is.
ROZ: Mm-hmm. It is not warm in here, baby. Take the napkin.
ELEANOR: More?
FAITH: Yes, ma'am. One more. Somebody sat down with me.
[END STATE] Faith has been seated at table nine by the man who owns it, has accepted the training role and the tuition, and has refused to cry over any of it. She cries when Eleanor feeds her one spoonful with the bent spoon.

## SCENE TWENTY-EIGHT: THE FIRST CLASS

[SCENE CONTEXT] The following Thursday, the old dining room, now a training floor. Twenty-two aides have enrolled and Vivian is sitting in the back row. Julian is a student today. Junie has been brought along because there was nobody to watch her. Knowledge state: Julian still believes the three taps are a memory. He has not answered them yet.
[SCENE PURPOSE] Return the lesson through the smallest character in the story, and pay off the childhood signal and the misheard name in the same scene.
FAITH: Chairs in a circle, everybody, not rows. Rows are how you end up behind somebody.
FAITH: Twenty-two signed up, so twenty-two chairs. And somebody is already sitting in the back row.
FAITH: Okay, everybody. We start with the wrong way. Stand behind the chair, reach over the shoulder, the way you have all seen it done.
FAITH: Now try to see the person's eyes. You cannot. And neither can they.
FAITH: So sit down beside them instead. That is lesson one, and honestly it is the whole class.
JULIAN: Miss Dawson. Am I doing this correctly?
FAITH: Sir, your elbow is up at your ear.
JULIAN: Mm. It feels natural at my ear.
FAITH: It feels natural because you are in a hurry.
JULIAN: I am always in a hurry. That has been established fairly thoroughly.
ELEANOR: Too. Fast.
JULIAN: Sorry, Mother. Again.
JUNIE: You are doing it wrong, mister. Move over, I will show you.
FAITH: Junie Dawson. Get off that chair right now.
JUNIE: But he is going too fast, Mommy. He is going so fast.
JULIAN: Ha. She is right. Let her show me.
JUNIE: Elbow down. All the way down, down, down. Now you wait.
[The lesson passes to the smallest character in the story, and the billionaire takes instruction from a five year old.]
JULIAN: How long do I wait?
JUNIE: Until she opens. You do not go first. That is the rule.
JULIAN: Mother. I am waiting.
ELEANOR: Ha.
JULIAN: Wait. Did you hear that? She laughed. She actually laughed.
FAITH: Mm-hmm. That is the first time I have ever heard it.
JUNIE: Now say the words. Small bites, then rest. Say it back.
JULIAN: Small bites. Then rest.
ELEANOR: Slow. Good. Stay.
JULIAN: I am staying, Mother.
ELEANOR: Tap. Tap. Tap. Three.
JULIAN: Oh. I know that one. Tap. Tap. Tap.
ELEANOR: You answered me.
[Recovery beat: her first complete sentence in the script. Earned by the doctor's line in scene twenty-two.]
JULIAN: Mother. That was a whole sentence.
ELEANOR: Doctor. Made me. Practice.
JULIAN: Weeks late. I am sorry.
ELEANOR: You still answered.
FAITH: All right, everybody, find a partner. Yes, back row. You have your hand up.
VIVIAN: Where do the pliers go when a person is finished with them?
FAITH: Back in the drawer, ma'am. Anybody can borrow them. That is the point of a drawer.
ELEANOR: Faith.
FAITH: Yes, ma'am?
ELEANOR: Faith. That is. Your name.
[Payoff of scene twenty. The word Julian heard as religion was a woman's name, and his mother had been saying it every Thursday.]
FAITH: It is. You have never once said it out loud.
ELEANOR: I have. Every Thursday. Nobody. Understood me.
JULIAN: I stood in that room and I thought she was talking about religion. I did not ask her once.
FAITH: Say it one more time, ma'am. Slow.
ELEANOR: Faith. Sat down. With me.
JULIAN: Miss Dawson. There is one more thing out in the lobby.
[END STATE] Julian has tapped back. Eleanor has produced her first complete sentence and has reclaimed Faith's name in front of the room, which retroactively explains everything she was saying in scene twenty.

## SCENE TWENTY-NINE: THE HOTEL LOBBY

[SCENE CONTEXT] Immediately after, the hotel lobby. The drawing from scene one has been framed and hung. The bent spoon is in a glass case beside it. Knowledge state: Junie is seeing her own work on a wall for the first time and does not understand why. Julian understands exactly why and says so.
[SCENE PURPOSE] Close the secondary symbol and let Julian state the story's moral cost in one line without moralising.
JUNIE: Mommy, why is everybody looking at the wall?
FAITH: Because there is something new on it, baby.
JUNIE: That is a picture. Wait. Wait, that is my picture. In a gold frame?
[The drawing from scene one closes its loop. It has travelled from a daycare gate to a hotel lobby wall.]
JULIAN: In a gold frame.
JUNIE: And that is my name in the corner. I wrote it myself, nobody helped me.
JULIAN: You did. Although the letter E is backwards.
JUNIE: It is not backwards. It is looking the other way.
JULIAN: Ha. My mistake entirely.
FAITH: And the little glass box next to it? What is that?
JULIAN: Read the card underneath it.
FAITH: One bent spoon. Property of Eleanor Whitfield. Not for return.
JUNIE: Mommy, why is a spoon in a box?
FAITH: Because somebody decided it was important, baby.
JULIAN: Miss Dawson. May I tell you something? That drawing hung on my mother's wall since March. And I stood in front of it three times.
FAITH: I know. I gave it to her the last Thursday in March.
JULIAN: Three times, and I never once asked what it was.
[The moral cost stated plainly by the man who could afford everything except a question.]
FAITH: Well. You asked in the end.
JULIAN: Mm. I asked in the end. That is not the same as asking in time.
FAITH: Then teach the next man to ask sooner.
JULIAN: Hah. That is what Thursdays are for now.
JUNIE: Mister. Can I ask a question?
JULIAN: You may.
JUNIE: Who are the two people in my picture?
JULIAN: You know, I have been wondering that myself all week.
JUNIE: Mommy said I get to pick. So one is your mom.
JULIAN: And the other one?
JUNIE: Anybody who sits down.
[The child answers the thematic question of the script without knowing there was one.]
FAITH: Baby. Get your coat. We have somewhere to be at five.
[END STATE] Julian has admitted he stood in front of the drawing three times and never asked. Junie has decided who the two people in her picture are: his mother, and anybody who sits down.

## SCENE THIRTY: THE DAYCARE GATE AT DUSK

[SCENE CONTEXT] That evening, the daycare gate from scene one. Faith is twenty minutes early for the first time in five years. The rent is paid, the daycare balance is cleared, and there is money left in the envelope. Knowledge state: Junie knows nothing about any of it. She wants to know what her mother did at work today.
[SCENE PURPOSE] Circular close. Same gate, same people, reversed conditions, and the entire story compressed into two words.
LORRAINE: Twenty minutes early. In five years I have never seen that once.
FAITH: Ha. They had to unlock the gate for me, Mama.
JUNIE: Mommy. Mommy, you are first. You beat everybody.
[Circular reversal of scene one, where Faith was late and running.]
FAITH: I did. Come here, you.
JUNIE: Look at my shoes. They do not have any tape on them, and they squeak on the floor.
FAITH: Well, that is how you know they are new.
FAITH: Come here, you. Mama, what is in the thermos?
LORRAINE: Warm milk. She started asking for it at four o'clock and she did not stop.
JUNIE: I need my spoon, Grandma. Not that one. My bent one.
FAITH: Here, baby. Stir it slow.
JUNIE: It goes around better when the handle is bent. It does, it really does.
LORRAINE: Mm. That is what she says every night now. Every single night.
FAITH: Ha. Let her say it. Mama, I paid Bright Lane this morning. All of it, the late fees too.
[Both money threads planted in scenes one and sixteen close here, quietly and without a total.]
LORRAINE: Mm-hmm. And the rent?
FAITH: Paid. And there is still something left in the envelope.
LORRAINE: Well. Would you look at that.
LORRAINE: Faith. You look different this week.
FAITH: I am just tired, Mama.
LORRAINE: Mm-hmm. That is not the different I meant.
JUNIE: Mommy. What did you do today? At the big hotel, what did you do?
FAITH: I sat down.
[The act that cost her the job is now the entire answer, and it costs her nothing.]
JUNIE: That is all?
FAITH: That is all, baby.
JUNIE: Did anybody yell at you?
FAITH: Not one person.
LORRAINE: Nobody yelled at her, sweetheart. Not anymore.
JUNIE: Grandma, why are your eyes wet?
LORRAINE: Oh. It is the cold, that is all. Drink your milk.
JUNIE: Mommy. Are you tired?
FAITH: I am. Small bites. Then rest.
[END STATE] The family is out of danger. Faith answers the question with the exact act that cost her the job, and the answer no longer costs her anything.

## CLOSING MESSAGE

NARRATOR: She never asked anybody for anything. She pulled out a chair, and she sat down, and it cost her the job she could not afford to lose. Kindness rarely arrives with a receipt. But somebody is always writing it down, and one day a person finally asks the right question.
