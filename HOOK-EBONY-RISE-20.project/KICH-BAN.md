## HOOK

Adrian Owens walked into Precinct 34 in a fresh officer's uniform.

"I've just been transferred here. Officer Wells."

Sergeant Bryce Hollister gave him a sour, appraising look, eyeing him from head to toe. "We didn't put in a request for a transfer."

"The Chief signed off on it, Sarge."

Hollister sighed, grimaced, and tossed a broom down at Adrian's feet. "The break room needs sweeping. That's your job."

A few officers chuckled. No one spoke up to object.

Adrian picked up the broom. His jaw tightened—but he maintained his cover. Tucked against his ribs, beneath his uniform shirt, was a captain's badge bearing his name.

Captain Adrian Owens was going undercover as a rookie officer in the very precinct he commanded. Every insult, every order—he took it all in.

And what he uncovered over the next two weeks... no one saw coming.

## PHẦN SAU HOOK — chỉ dùng lấy ngữ cảnh, bối cảnh và nhân vật chuẩn

Precinct 34 had been open for forty years. In those four decades, every single captain who had ever sat behind the corner office desk shared one thing in common. And it had nothing to do with skill, rank, or service record.

The hallway leading to the captain's office told the story plainly. Framed portraits hung in a row along the wall — twelve captains, twelve faces, forty years. Every one of them looked exactly the same kind of person. Not a single exception. A brass plaque beneath each photo listed their name, their years of service, and not much else. But anyone walking that hallway would notice the pattern without reading a single word.

Then the Ridgemont Police Department signed an appointment letter that would break that pattern for the first time. Captain Adrian Owens — twenty-two years in law enforcement, decorated service record, zero disciplinary marks, promoted through every rank the hard way. He was the first person who didn't match those twelve portraits to ever be given command of Precinct 34.

The announcement hadn't been made yet. The paperwork sat in the Chief's office, unsigned by the final countersignature, when Adrian received something that changed everything.

A letter. No return address. No signature. The handwriting was shaky — not careless, but afraid. Whoever wrote it had pressed the pen so hard in places that the paper was nearly torn through.

The letter described a system. Not one bad officer. A system — built over years, maintained through silence, enforced through fear. Officers who didn't fit the mold were given the worst shifts. Complaints from the community disappeared from the filing system. Reports were altered. Evidence was fabricated.

So Adrian took his captain's badge, folded it carefully in a piece of cloth, and tucked it into the inner pocket of a standard-issue patrol officer's shirt. He registered at Precinct 34 under the name Officer A. Wells.

Over five days he documented Officer Neil Crenshaw's thirty-seven unlawful stops, Officer Derek Sloan's fourteen fabricated arrest reports, and finally Sergeant Bryce Hollister on tape ordering the fabrication of evidence and threatening the whistleblower, Officer Tamara Bishop.

On the Friday morning, in front of forty-three officers, he unbuttoned his shirt, pinned the captain's badge to his chest and said: "My name is not Wells. I am Captain Adrian Owens. Your new commanding officer." All three men were suspended within the hour. Tamara Bishop was promoted, and later made Sergeant. Three wrongfully convicted men walked free.
