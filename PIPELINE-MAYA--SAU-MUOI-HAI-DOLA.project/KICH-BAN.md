MAYA: She gets a mother with a job.
MAYA: That is everything, ma'am. Put your coat on, baby, we are going.
[END STATE] Preston keeps a desk and loses the succession permanently. Maya has taken the job and refused the check.

## SCENE TWENTY NINE: THE COFFEE CAN, AGAIN
[SCENE CONTEXT] Months later, a new two bedroom apartment leased in Maya's own name. The ring was redeemed on day eighty three with her first paycheck.
[SCENE PURPOSE] Closing beat. Show that everything was earned, not given, and refill the coffee can.
MAYA: The lease is in my name, Gwen. My name, on the top line, in my own handwriting.
GWEN: It is your mother's, is what it is. Did that woman pay for this?
[Nothing in this apartment was given to her]
MAYA: No. First paycheck, every dollar of it, and I did not tell her I was doing it.
GWEN: Lord. This family. Both of you are exactly the same.
LILY: Gwen, listen. Listen to the can when I shake it.
GWEN: What am I listening for, baby?
LILY: The coins. It has nineteen dollars in it again, and some pennies underneath.
GWEN: Nineteen dollars. And who are you saving up for this time?
LILY: The lady who only had water. I am taking her out.
LILY: To the same place and the same table. Mama already telephoned about it.
GWEN: And who is paying for this dinner, may I ask?
LILY: Me. It is my can and it is my money.
MAYA: She has been counting it in her sleep. I hear her doing it through the wall.
GWEN: Baby, nineteen dollars will not cover three dinners in a restaurant.
MAYA: Put on your coat, baby. Table is at six.
[END STATE] The can is filling again and this time the child is saving to feed somebody else.

## SCENE THIRTY: TABLE FOUR
[SCENE CONTEXT] The Copper Kettle, table four, the same hour of the evening. Three of them are eating together. Two tables over, a man is sitting alone with a glass of water and melted ice.
[SCENE PURPOSE] Bookend. Reverse the mother's line from scene two and hand the motif to the next stranger.
ROSEANNE: Three of you tonight? Well, look at this.
MARGARET: Roseanne, give the child the check when it comes. Directly into her hand.
MARGARET: She has been saving up since the spring. Do not embarrass her in front of her mother.
ROSEANNE: Understood, ma'am. I will bring it on the little tray.
MARGARET: That has been true since the first evening I met her.
LILY: And pie after?
MAYA: There is always enough for pie now.
MARGARET: Two pies, Roseanne. I am not sitting through that again.
LILY: Mama. Listen.
MAYA: ...To what, baby?
LILY: That man over by the window. He was here before we came in.
MARGARET: Which man is that, child?
[Same table, same hour, same words the child used in scene two]
LILY: The one on his own. Nobody has looked at him one time since we sat down.
LILY: Mama, that man has only water.
MAYA: Is the ice gone out of it?
LILY: The ice is all gone.
MARGARET: Take the clip, child. He is going to want proof.
LILY: Mama? Can I?
[The reversal of her line in scene two]
MAYA: Go on, baby. That is our business now.
[END STATE] The pattern repeats and the mother does not hesitate. The clip goes to somebody new.

## CLOSING
This is the story of a woman who had sixty two dollars and gave away the last of it, and lost almost everything for fifteen minutes of decency. Kindness is not what is left over after the bills are paid. It is the thing a person pays for, and the paying is what makes it real. Somewhere close by, someone is waiting for one person to stop.
