Cop Tackled Black Man at Airport — Went Pale When He's Federal Judge Who Reviews Police Cases

"Get your hands off me — I just asked which gate goes to Chicago!"
"Shut your mouth. You people stink up this whole terminal. Back up before I drop you right here."
"Officer, I don't want trouble. I'm just trying to catch my flight."
"Flight? You look like you crawled out of a shelter. Who even let you past security?"
That was Officer Craig Brennan — badge on his chest, sneer on his face — talking to a Black man who had done nothing but ask a question.
The man's name was Warren. Hands visible. Voice low. "I'm a federal employee, sir. Here's my boarding pass."
Brennan slapped the phone out of his hand. "I don't care what's on your little screen. A guy like you walking around with platinum cards? Where'd you steal those?"
A woman nearby started recording.
Brennan yanked Warren's bag open and dumped everything. Legal folders, a dress shirt, a turkey sandwich — scattered across the floor. He held up a gavel-shaped paperweight and laughed. "What is this? You playing lawyer now?"
"I don't consent to this search. I have rights under the Fourth Amendment."
Brennan's face went red. His captain arrived, took one look at Warren, and said, "One more word about rights and you're in handcuffs."
Then Brennan patted him down. When Warren's hand moved toward his jacket pocket, Brennan screamed "GUN!" and slammed him face-first into the tile. Cheekbone cracked. Blood from his lip. Handcuffs snapped shut.
A little girl whispered, "Daddy, why is that man on the floor?"
Nobody answered.
Then Brennan's partner looked down. A leather ID holder had fallen open. She picked it up. Read it. And went white.
Gold seal. Raised black letters. The Honorable Warren J. Foster. United States Court of Appeals Judge. Specialty — police misconduct. Excessive force. Civil rights violations.
The exact kind of case being born on that airport floor.
Brennan's hands started trembling. The captain fumbled with the handcuffs. Warren stood up slowly — blood on his lip, bruise on his cheek, and a look in his eyes that said everything was about to change.
But what Warren said next, while the whole terminal watched — you don't want to miss that. Part two is coming.
