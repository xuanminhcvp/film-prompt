Bank Manager Slapped a Black Intern on Day One: "You'll Never Last" —She Was the Chairman's Daughter

"Who let this stray in? Somebody call the pound — we got a Black dog loose on my floor."
"I'm your new intern, sir. I was assigned to this branch —"
"Intern? You? Look at you. You look like you crawled out of a shelter. We don't put animals behind desks."
"I graduated summa cum laude, sir. I'm here to learn."
"Summa cum laude?" He laughed so loud the whole floor heard it. "What school hands out degrees to strays now? Some little affirmative action handout so they could check a box?"
"Howard University, sir. I earned my place."
"Earned?" Trent Holloway stepped forward until he was inches from her face. "You didn't earn anything. You're a charity case. A diversity hire. A warm body to make corporate feel progressive."
He swung his open palm straight across her face. The crack echoed through the trading floor like a gunshot. Every keyboard stopped. Every phone call paused.
"You'll never last here, Black girl. Not one week."
Imani pressed her hand to her burning cheek. She didn't step back. She didn't cry. She looked him dead in the eye and said nothing.
That silence would cost him everything.
For three weeks, Holloway made it his mission to break her. Locked her in a supply closet. Made her mop floors, scrub toilets, haul trash through the lobby in her blazer. Banned the staff from speaking to her. Blocked her email. Stripped her badge. When she filed a complaint with HR, the head of Human Resources — Holloway's fraternity brother — killed it in seventy-two hours. No interviews. No investigation. Three words: insufficient evidence.
She showed up every single day.
Then one Monday, a name appeared on the branch calendar. R. Davis — Chairman's Office.
Holloway spent the weekend pressing his best suit. He had no idea what was coming.
Raymond Davis stepped off the elevator at nine sharp. Gathered the full staff. Forty-one employees. Including Imani.
He asked one question: "How's your intern program going?"
Holloway smiled. "She's been a challenge, sir. Attitude problems."
Raymond nodded. Then his assistant placed a tablet on the table and pressed play. Security footage. Day one. The slap. Every word. Every second.
Holloway's face drained white.
Raymond looked at him and said five words that changed everything.
But what those five words were — and what happened to Holloway after — that's Part 2. Follow so you don't miss it.
