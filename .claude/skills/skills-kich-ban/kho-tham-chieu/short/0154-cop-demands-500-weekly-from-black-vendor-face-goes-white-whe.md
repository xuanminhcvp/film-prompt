Cop Demands $500 Weekly From Black Vendor — Face Goes White When 'Vendor' Pulls Out FBI Badge

"Five hundred a week. That's what it costs for a filthy Black boy like you to stink up my sidewalk with your greasy little cart."
"I have all my permits right here, officer. Everything's legal."
"Legal? You think a piece of paper makes you belong here? Look at you — sweating over a trash can full of charcoal like some kind of animal. Your kind doesn't build anything. You just squat on corners and drag the whole block down."
"I'm just trying to make a living, sir."
"A living? My shoes cost more than everything you own. Now shut your mouth and listen."
Officer Craig Dawson grabbed a rib straight off Byron's grill, bit into it, and spit the bone right onto the food. Then he leaned in close.
"Five hundred. Cash. Every Friday. Or I'll have this garbage heap towed and you face-down on concrete. Don't even think about crying to anyone — nobody gives a damn about another broke Black man with a grill."
Byron wiped his hands slow. "Understood, officer."
And he paid. Every Friday. White envelope. Five hundred dollars. Then Dawson raised it to seven-fifty.
Eleven vendors of color had already vanished from this block. A Jamaican patty stand. A Salvadoran pupusa cart. A jewelry maker who worked four years straight. All gone. White vendors on the same sidewalk? Dawson never touched them.
Renee — sixty years old, sold flowers twenty-two years — told Byron she had to choose between paying Dawson and buying her medication. She was shutting down.
Byron listened. Said nothing. Just glanced at his watch.
That watch. Nobody looked twice at it. But that cheap-looking digital watch on Byron's wrist was doing something no one on Fulton Avenue knew about.
And those envelopes Byron handed over every Friday? The cash inside had a secret too.
Week ten. Dawson rolled up. Hand out. Same smirk. Same swagger.
But this time, Byron didn't reach for an envelope. He reached for something else — something that would turn Dawson's face white and his whole world upside down.
What Byron pulled out next changed everything. But that's for Part Two.
