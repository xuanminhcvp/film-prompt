Black Driver Performs 6 Minutes of CPR on Woman in Rain— What Happened After Changed His Entire Life

A man with thirty-eight dollars in his bank account is about to make a decision that will change his entire life. But right now, he doesn't know that. Right now, he's just tired.
Andre Hill. Rideshare driver. Fourteen hours behind the wheel. Parked on a quiet downtown street, about to log off and go home.
Then he sees her.
A woman steps out of a wine bar. Blonde hair. Silk blouse. An emerald brooch at her throat worth more than Andre makes in five years. She takes two steps — and drops. Face against wet concrete. She doesn't move.
Two people walk past. A man nearby stares at his phone. Nobody helps.
Andre looks through his rain-streaked windshield. He's a Black man. She's an unconscious white woman. Dark street. Night. He knows exactly how this could go wrong for him.
He gets out anyway.
Knees on wet pavement. Two fingers on her neck. Pulse — fading. Lips turning blue.
He starts CPR. Alone. In the pouring rain. Knuckles splitting against concrete. Arms shaking.
One minute. Nothing. Two minutes. Nothing. Three minutes. Her face is turning gray.
A crowd gathers. Phones out. Recording. Not one person steps forward.
Four minutes. Five. His body is giving out. He hasn't eaten in six hours.
He keeps pushing.
"Stay with me. Come on. Stay with me."
Minute six. She coughs. A raw, desperate gasp. Her eyes fly open. She pulls in a breath so deep it sounds like someone surfacing from underwater.
She's alive. Because of him.
The paramedic shakes his head. "Another minute and she'd be gone."
Andre goes home. Soaking wet. Hands torn up. Still thirty-eight dollars in his account. He falls asleep thinking that's the end of it.
But here's what Andre doesn't know.
The entire time — across the street — a man in a dark suit was watching from a black Escalade. And the moment Andre started CPR, that man made a phone call.
Because the woman Andre just saved? She's not just anyone. She's the daughter of one of the most powerful billionaires on the East Coast.
And by six the next morning, three people were already looking for Andre Hill.
