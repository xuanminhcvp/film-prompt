"Get this street monkey off my runway before I have him arrested!"
"Sir, please — the fuel coupling on your left engine is cracked. If you fire that turbine —"
"A janitor. Is telling ME. About turbines." Bellmont jabbed his finger into Darnell's chest. "You can barely read your own badge, boy. You think you belong near my aircraft?"
"I just need someone to look at —"
"Security! Get this trash off my tarmac. Now."
Two guards dragged Darnell behind the cordon like a criminal.
Darnell Tucker was nineteen. A temp worker at Aerocore Dynamics — a defense contractor weeks from a sixty-million-dollar flight test in front of the Pentagon.
His badge had one word in red. TEMP.
Three weeks ago, on a night shift, Darnell spotted what no engineer had caught. A hairline fracture on the fuel coupling of the Sentinel-4's left engine.
He filed a report. Nobody read it.
He tried again through a technician named Rena. She brought it to Craig Bellmont — Senior Flight Engineer. Eighteen years. Degrees on the wall. Parking spot with his name on concrete.
Bellmont saw who wrote the report and shoved it aside. "A janitor writing maintenance reports? Trash it."
So on test day, when Darnell saw the fracture had grown — saw fuel already seeping through metal — he crossed the line. Begged them to stop.
Bellmont laughed.
"Fire it up."
Ten seconds.
Every alarm screamed. White foam swallowed the engine. The turbine shrieked and died. Red lights spinning. Pentagon brass stumbling backward.
Bellmont stood frozen. Headset dangling. Career evaporating in real time.
But here's what no one understood yet.
That engine blowing on the ground didn't just prove Darnell right.
It saved every person standing there.
Because what Darnell found hidden inside that engine next made the cracked coupling look like nothing.
And what it revealed about Bellmont ended everything.
