Cop Steals $189K from Black Drivers for 14 Years — Until He Stops a Black Man Driving Undercover

"Get out of the car, boy. You stink like food stamps and Section 8."
"Officer, I'm happy to cooperate. I'm the newly appointed Director at the Department of—"
"Director?" Hollister slammed the roof. "The only thing you're directing is a welfare line. Hands where I can see them before I drag you out myself."
The man stepped out slowly. Hands up.
Hollister shoved him toward the back of the car. "Gallagher, search it. Every Black guy in a nice car out here is either stealing it or running dope."
"Sir, this is a federal vehicle. The registration will confirm—"
"Shut your mouth." Hollister grabbed the envelope from the console. Two thousand dollars. His eyes lit up. "Drug money. You people always got an excuse."
"That's my personal money. I can prove—"
"You can prove it to the county. If you can read the paperwork." He tossed the cash to Gallagher. "Bag it."
Then he pointed at the ground. "Sit. Right there. On the gravel."
Ninety-three degrees. No shade. The man sat without a word.
Hollister pulled out his phone, laughing. "Yeah, another one. Two grand. Easiest money all week."
For fourteen years, Sergeant Craig Hollister had run this highway like a personal toll booth. Wait for a Black driver. Pull them over. Take their cash. No charges. No arrests. A hundred and eighty-nine thousand dollars stolen from people who couldn't fight back.
He handed the man a citation and leaned in. "Drive safe now, boy. Next time, stay in your lane. You don't belong on this highway."
The man didn't yell. Didn't argue. He picked up his phone and made one call. Ninety seconds.
Within the hour, two black Suburbans with federal plates were tearing toward Colter County.
Because the man on that gravel — the man Hollister called "boy," the man he said didn't belong — had just been confirmed by the United States Senate as the Director of the FBI.
And Hollister? He was already back in his cruiser, scanning for his next target. No idea that the last fourteen years of his life were about to collapse underneath him.
