Sheriff Cuffed Two Black Men in a Diner: "Welcome to My Town" — One Was His New Police Chief Monday

"Get up. Now. Animals don't eat where people sit."
"Sir, we paid for our meal. We're just having breakfast—"
"Did I stutter? You reek. You're making these good people lose their appetite. Stray dogs belong outside, not in a booth."
"We don't want any trouble—"
"Trouble? Things like you don't get choices in my town."
Sheriff Braddock grabbed the man by the collar and yanked him out of the booth. His coffee mug shattered on the floor. His friend jumped up — the deputy slammed him face-first into the table. Blood hit the tile before the plates did.
Cuffs on both. Face down on the cold floor.
Braddock stood over them. Grinning.
"Welcome to my town. And in my town — I decide who's human."
Twelve people watched. Not one said a word.
But behind the pastry case, the waitress had her phone out. Hands shaking. Recording everything.
In the kitchen, the diner owner picked up a phone the sheriff didn't know about — and called the mayor.
Braddock grabbed his radio. "Dispatch. Two suspects. Possible narcotics." There were no narcotics. No crime. He made it all up.
His deputy broke into their rental SUV. Suitcases thrown on gravel. A leather briefcase pried open with a knife.
Inside — an FBI commendation letter. A signed appointment from the mayor's office. And at the bottom, wrapped in cloth — a chief of police badge.
The deputy didn't read a word. Tossed it in the dirt.
Then a black town car pulled into the lot.
Mayor Whitfield pushed through the door. Saw the man on the floor — hands cuffed behind his back — and the color drained from her face.
"Take those handcuffs off. Right now."
Braddock smirked. "Mayor, this is law enforcement—"
"That man on your floor is Isaiah Davis. The new Chief of Police of this county. He starts Monday. And you just arrested him for ordering pancakes."
Braddock's face went white. His mouth opened. Nothing came out.
Isaiah stood up slowly. Dusted off his jeans. Looked him dead in the eye.
"You said you decide who's human. As of Monday — every badge in this county answers to me. Including yours."
But this story's far from over. What they found buried in eighteen years of files — that's where it really gets ugly. Part two is up now.
