## SCENE 1: THE COUNTER

[SCENE CONTEXT]
TIME: Tuesday afternoon, appliance floor of Halvorsen Home and Electric.
KNOWLEDGE STATE: Bryce believes Walter is an easy mark, an old man shopping alone. Walter does not know he is being cheated. Maya knows the box carries five years of factory coverage. Nobody knows who Walter really is, including the audience.
STATE: Maya is three metres away with a mop. She is twenty eight, has worked here eight years, and has eleven days of her mother's insulin at home. She cannot afford to be noticed.

[SCENE PURPOSE]
Open on the act of kindness and finish it inside one scene. Establish the fraud in plain words, show that Maya speaks knowing the cost, and end with her withholding her name, which is the knot the whole story hangs on.

BRYCE: Sign at the bottom, sir. And put your initials in the box.
WALTER: What am I signing?
BRYCE: Protection.
WALTER: Protection for the machine?
BRYCE: Protection for you. Three thousand.
WALTER: That is more than the washer costs.
BRYCE: It is peace of mind. The pen is by your hand.
MAYA: Sir. Look at me for one second.
[INTENTION: she has been listening for a full minute; this is a decision, not an impulse]
BRYCE: We are in the middle of something.
MAYA: You do not need that. It is already covered. Five years, from the factory, printed on the box.
BRYCE: That is manufacturer coverage. This is a separate product.
MAYA: It is the same coverage. He would be buying it twice.
BRYCE: You are a cleaner.
[BEAT: Bryce abandons the sale and attacks her standing instead]
MAYA: I know what I am. I break down those boxes every night.
WALTER: Is she right?
BRYCE: Sir, this is a value added service.
WALTER: I asked you a question. Is she right?
MAYA: Read him line four. Out loud.
BRYCE: I do not have to read you anything.
MAYA: Then I will. Coverage begins when the manufacturer term expires. That means year six, sir. You are paying three thousand dollars for year six.
WALTER: Three thousand dollars for year six.
[KNOWLEDGE: Walter understands the fraud for the first time]
MAYA: For nothing. Small print is still print.
WALTER: Why are you telling me this?
MAYA: Because you walked in here by yourself.
WALTER: That young man is going to make trouble for you. Step back. I will sign it and go, and nothing happens to you.
[KNOWLEDGE: he understands the price before she does]
MAYA: No, sir. Put the pen down.
WALTER: And if I do not?
MAYA: Then I will stand here until you do. I am not moving.
WALTER: All right. The pen is on the counter. What is your name?
MAYA: I just work here, sir.
WALTER: That is not a name.
MAYA: It is the part that matters today. Go to register four and ask for Dorothy. She will not push anything on you.
[MISUNDERSTANDING: she withholds her name to stay invisible, which is exactly what will make her impossible to find]
WALTER: And what about you?
BRYCE: Halloway. Yes. Aisle three, appliances. Right now.
WALTER: Who is he calling?
MAYA: Somebody who is going to ask me why I opened my mouth.

[END STATE]
The sale is dead and Bryce has lost his biggest commission. Walter leaves without her name, so he will not be able to find her later. Maya has been called to aisle three.

## SCENE 2: THE BREAK ROOM

[BEAT: the fear surfaces for the first time]

[END STATE]
Eleven days of her mother's insulin and insurance tied to a badge are on the table. Maya states she will not deny what she did. Her name goes out over the loudspeaker.

GRANT: It is today, Fletcher. Put the badge on the shelf, face down.
MAYA: My mother's insurance is on this badge.
GRANT: Then you should have thought about your mother before you opened your mouth.
GRANT: Nothing to say?
MAYA: Show me where to sign.

[END STATE]
Maya is fired with sabotage of sales in her file, which will block unemployment and every future job. Walter has been humiliated and removed. Neither knows the other's name.

ELLIS: You were in the hospital. Then Mom died. Then you were in the hospital again, and somebody had to run it. The board wanted margin, the stores had none, and the only margin in retail is attachment. I did not invent it.
[KNOWLEDGE: his motive is grief and fear, not greed]
WALTER: You approved it.
ELLIS: I kept nine thousand people employed while you were grieving. Somebody in this family had to be the adult.
WALTER: You kept it alive on people who came in alone.
ELLIS: That is not fair.
WALTER: That is exactly what it is. Say it once and I will never bring it up again.
ELLIS: There is a state hearing in eleven days.
WALTER: I am aware of it.
ELLIS: Then be aware of this as well. Your witness has a file. Sabotage of sales. Theft adjacent conduct, unresolved.
[INTENTION: a threat dressed as information]
WALTER: That is a lie.
ELLIS: It is a record, Dad. There is a difference, and the difference is what hearings run on.

[END STATE]
Ellis reveals the altered file and effectively threatens the witness. The hearing is eleven days away.

## SCENE 24: THE HEARING PREPARATION

[SCENE CONTEXT]
TIME: Two days before the hearing.
KNOWLEDGE STATE: Maya has sixty two names but has never read line nineteen closely. Marguerite knows the sworn statement will discredit her.
STATE: Bryce has signed against her.

[SCENE PURPOSE]
The discovery. Turn the antagonist's weapon into the thing that destroys him.

MARGUERITE: Sixty two accounts. Names, dates, amounts.
MAYA: How many of them will talk?
MARGUERITE: Eleven so far. Which is eleven more than they are expecting.
RUTHANN: That poor man from the pantry is bringing his daughter with him.
MAYA: He was frightened of her taking his checkbook.
RUTHANN: And now she is the one driving him.
MARGUERITE: There is a problem, though.
MAYA: Say it.
MARGUERITE: Bryce Lundgren signed a statement yesterday. Sworn.
MAYA: Saying what?
MARGUERITE: That you had a personal grievance against him, interrupted his sale out of spite, and have been recruiting complaints ever since. It is clean, it is specific, and it makes every name on your list look purchased.
MAYA: He knows that is not true.
MARGUERITE: He knows what a closure list is. They showed him one.
[KNOWLEDGE: Bryce was coerced the same way Grant was]
MAYA: Let me see the accounts again.
MARGUERITE: Maya, we should be preparing your testimony.
MAYA: One minute. Line nine. Line twelve. Line nineteen.
MARGUERITE: What is it?
MAYA: Line nineteen.
[BEAT: the discovery; note she goes quiet rather than loud]
MARGUERITE: Lundgren, C. What about it?
MAYA: It is the same last name.
MARGUERITE: It is a common name.
MAYA: Same chain. Different branch. The county road store.
MARGUERITE: Four months ago. A refrigerator. Three thousand and change, financed.
RUTHANN: Lord have mercy.
MAYA: Ruthann, where is my coat?
RUTHANN: Maya, wait a minute.
MAYA: Where does he park?
[INTENTION: deliberately ambiguous; the audience should expect revenge]

[END STATE]
Maya finds Colleen Lundgren on her own list and leaves to find Bryce, without saying what she intends to do.

## SCENE 25: THE PARKING GARAGE

[SCENE CONTEXT]
TIME: That evening, parking garage under the store.
KNOWLEDGE STATE: Maya knows his mother is a victim. Bryce does not. He expects to be threatened or blackmailed.
STATE: She is holding one page and nothing else.

[SCENE PURPOSE]
Confrontation through empathy. She defeats him by refusing to ask him for anything.

BRYCE: You cannot be here.
MAYA: It is a public garage.
BRYCE: I have nothing to say to you.
MAYA: That is fine. I have one thing to say and then I will go.
BRYCE: They made me sign that statement. Halloway put the closure list on the desk in front of me.
MAYA: I know he did. Read line nineteen.
BRYCE: What is this?
MAYA: It is the account list. Sixty two people. Read line nineteen.
BRYCE: I am not going to stand in a garage and read your list.
MAYA: Read it.
BRYCE: Lundgren, C. County road. Three thousand and change. No.
[KNOWLEDGE: payoff of the delivery confirmation from scene 12]
MAYA: Say it out loud.
BRYCE: That is my mother.
MAYA: Four months ago. Ninety one dollars a month for thirty six months.
BRYCE: She asked me on the phone if she needed it, and I told her I was busy. She called me about a hospital bill last month. Eight hundred dollars, and she is paying ninety one a month for nothing.
MAYA: Somebody just did to her what you do all day. Some young man your age, at another store, hitting his number.
BRYCE: What do you want from me?
MAYA: Nothing.
[INTENTION: refusing to make a demand is the entire strategy]
BRYCE: People do not come to a parking garage for nothing.
MAYA: That is the difference between us. I am not asking you for anything.
BRYCE: Then why are you standing here?
MAYA: Because somebody should have told you before it was your mother. Nobody did.
BRYCE: I was top of the district. Fourteen months running. They put my picture up in the break room.
MAYA: I mopped underneath it.
BRYCE: I told myself I never lied. Not once. I only ever answered what they asked me.
[BEAT: he tries the sentence from scene 12 and it no longer works]
MAYA: And you made sure they did not know what to ask.
BRYCE: That is not the same as lying.
MAYA: Say that sentence to her. Out loud. See how it sounds in her kitchen.
BRYCE: I cannot.
MAYA: That is what I thought.
MAYA: The pen is right there on your hood. Do whatever you want with it.
[MOTIF: the pen is offered rather than pushed, for the first time in the story]

[END STATE]
His self justification collapses. Nothing is agreed and no promise is made, which is why his choice at the hearing will mean something.

## SCENE 26: THE HALLWAY OUTSIDE THE HEARING ROOM

[SCENE CONTEXT]
TIME: Hearing day, minutes before the session. Hallway outside the room.
KNOWLEDGE STATE: Grant does not know Walter is in the building. He believes he is negotiating with a woman who has nothing left. Maya has not told anyone what she will ask for.
STATE: Both of them are about to go into the same room.

[SCENE PURPOSE]
Give the antagonist one last human plea and let her refuse it without cruelty. Withhold her terms so the boardroom scene still has a surprise.

MARGUERITE: They seat the complainant on the left. Do not stand up unless the chair asks you to.
MAYA: How long does it take?
MARGUERITE: Forty minutes, if nobody argues.
GRANT: Ms. Fletcher.
MAYA: Mr. Halloway.
GRANT: I am going to say one thing to you before we go in, and then I will not speak to you again.
MARGUERITE: My client is not talking to you in a hallway.
GRANT: It is not a threat. It is arithmetic. There are forty people on my payroll and most of them are twice your age.
[INTENTION: he is asking for mercy while refusing to call it that]
MAYA: I know their names. I started there at twenty and I mopped around them for eight years.
GRANT: Then you know what happens to that store if this goes badly today.
MAYA: I know what happened to me when it went well.
GRANT: You had one bad afternoon.
MAYA: I had eleven weeks. My mother counted her insulin in a refrigerator in somebody else's basement.
GRANT: That is not what I did to you.
MAYA: You wrote four words on a form and went home. That is exactly what you did to me.
[BEAT: the exact size of his crime named for the first time]
GRANT: They are going to ask you what you want out of this.
MAYA: They are.
GRANT: What are you going to say?
MAYA: I am going to say it in the room, Mr. Halloway. Not in a hallway where nobody writes it down.
[KNOWLEDGE: she has already decided her three terms; the audience does not learn them until scene 29]

[END STATE]
Grant has admitted out loud what he is afraid of. Maya has refused to negotiate anywhere that is not on the record.

## SCENE 27: THE HEARING

[SCENE CONTEXT]
TIME: Continuous. Inside the hearing room.
KNOWLEDGE STATE: The panel knows only the file. Grant does not know Walter is seated at the back. Maya knows and has said nothing.
STATE: Sixty two names in front of her.

[SCENE PURPOSE]
Let the antagonist commit fully to his position in public before it is destroyed.

CHAIR: This panel is reviewing consumer complaints regarding the Guardian Plus protection program. Ms. Fletcher, you may proceed.
MAYA: Sixty two names. Ninety one dollars a month. Eighty four dollars a month. One hundred and twelve dollars a month. A widow on Warren Street. A man who eats at a church pantry so that the payment clears. Every one of them bought coverage they already had, and every one of them was standing at that counter by themselves.
CHAIR: Mr. Halloway, what is the store's response?
GRANT: Our customers are always fully informed.
[INTENTION: he believes he is winning; note he never scans the room]
CHAIR: Fully informed.
GRANT: Every term is disclosed in the agreement. Every customer signs it.
MAYA: Then have him read the small print out loud. All of it. We have time.
[MOTIF: her line from scene 1 returned in a room with legal power]
GRANT: Mr. Chairman, this woman was separated from our company for cause.
CHAIR: I have her file. Sabotage of sales. Ms. Fletcher, what did you actually do?
MAYA: I told a man in an old coat that he already had five years of coverage.
GRANT: And it cost this store three thousand dollars in one afternoon.
CHAIR: Ms. Fletcher, do you have anything to add before I rule on the record?
MAYA: Only that the man in the old coat is sitting in the back of this room, and he has been raising his hand for two minutes.
[BEAT: cliffhanger into the next scene]

[END STATE]
Grant has put fully informed on the record and repeated the sabotage charge. Maya reveals that the man in the old coat is in the room.

## SCENE 28: THE HEARING, THE OWNER STANDS UP

[SCENE CONTEXT]
TIME: Continuous, seconds later, same room.
KNOWLEDGE STATE: The room is about to learn who Walter is. Nobody knows what Bryce decided in the garage, including the audience.
STATE: Grant is mid sentence.

[SCENE PURPOSE]
Power reversal and moral payoff in the same scene.

CHAIR: Sir, you are out of order. Identify yourself.
WALTER: Walter Kingsley. Kingsley Ridge Capital. I own the fund that owns this chain, and eleven weeks ago a young man in that store tried to sell me three thousand dollars of nothing.
[KNOWLEDGE: the reveal lands on Grant, Bryce and the panel at once]
CHAIR: Say that again for the record.
WALTER: I said I was not fully informed. I was standing right there and nobody informed me of anything except the price.

BRYCE: No. No, sir.
[BEAT: he makes the choice alone; nobody asked him to]
CHAIR: Do you understand the consequences of saying that?
BRYCE: Yes, sir. I understand them.

BRYCE: We were trained to find shoppers who came in alone. Widows, mostly. I was the best at it in the district.
CHAIR: Is that training written anywhere?
BRYCE: Two pages. It is called Engaging the Senior Shopper and it tells you to identify unaccompanied guests. It is in the onboarding binder in every store.

BRYCE: She was right. She was right in front of forty customers, and they walked her out the back door for it.

[END STATE]
