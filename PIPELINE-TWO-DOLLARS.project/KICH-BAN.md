## SCENE 1: THE DRIVEWAY

VANESSA: Owen. Wake up. Get out of that truck and take your bags.
OWEN: It's after midnight. What are you doing?
VANESSA: What I should have done four years ago. That's your gym bag. That's your work coat. That box is the junk from the garage.
OWEN: Those are my father's tools.
VANESSA: They're junk. They smell like junk. This whole house smells like you.
OWEN: Keep your voice down. The Hendersons are right there.
VANESSA: Let them look. Let every one of them look. I have spent six years walking into rooms beside a man with oil under his fingernails.
OWEN: I work. That's what oil under the nails means.
VANESSA: It means you're a mechanic. It means you'll always be a mechanic.
OWEN: I paid for this house.
VANESSA: You paid the small parts of it.
OWEN: I paid the mortgage every month for six years. I paid your mother's car note. I paid the taxes on a house I'm standing outside of at midnight.
VANESSA: And now you're done paying. Congratulations.
OWEN: Vanessa. Who's inside my house?
VANESSA: My house.
OWEN: Who is standing in my kitchen in my robe?
PRESTON: Evening, Owen.
OWEN: Preston.
PRESTON: You can call me Mr. Davis. You don't work for me, but let's keep some structure to the night.
OWEN: You're wearing my robe.
PRESTON: It was on the hook. It's soft. You have good taste in exactly one thing.
VANESSA: Don't talk to him. He's leaving.
OWEN: Six years. And you do it like this. In a driveway, in the dark, with him standing in the window.
VANESSA: I did it in the driveway so I wouldn't have to smell you in my living room one more night. Take your bags, Owen. Take them and go.

## SCENE 2: THE FRONT PORCH

VANESSA: Before you go, you're signing something.
OWEN: I'm not signing anything at one in the morning.
VANESSA: Then you're not getting the tools.
DIANE: Just sign it, Owen. Be a man about it for once.
OWEN: Diane. You're up too.
DIANE: I've been up. I've been waiting six years for tonight.
OWEN: Your camera's on. That little light by the door.
VANESSA: I know. I turned it on before I woke you up.
OWEN: Why would you record this?
VANESSA: Because in a year you'll be telling people I was cruel, and I want the tape.
DIANE: Vanessa.
VANESSA: What? Let him have a copy too. I don't care. Put the paper on the hood. Sign there, and there.
OWEN: What is this?
VANESSA: A final settlement. Brent wrote it. It's clean.
OWEN: This says I give up all claim to the house.
VANESSA: Correct.
OWEN: The savings. The truck loan. All of it.
VANESSA: Correct.
OWEN: And this. Neither party shall have any claim on property, income, or assets acquired by the other after the date of separation.
VANESSA: That's standard language.
DIANE: He can't even read it. Look at him squinting at a truck hood.
OWEN: I read fine, Diane. I'm reading the part where your daughter keeps everything.
VANESSA: I earned everything.
OWEN: You earned it while I worked nights so you could stand in open houses on Sunday.
VANESSA: Sign it.
OWEN: And what do I get?
VANESSA: You want your share? Here's your share.
OWEN: You just counted two dollars into my hand.
VANESSA: One. Two. Done. That is exactly what six years with you was worth. Buy yourself something.
DIANE: Buy a bus ticket.
OWEN: Fine. There. It's signed.
VANESSA: Was that so hard?
OWEN: One day you're going to remember this porch.
VANESSA: I'm going to remodel this porch. Goodbye, Owen.

## SCENE 3: ANDRE'S GARAGE

ANDRE: Owen. Owen, wake up. Why are you sleeping in the truck?

OWEN: Andre —
ANDRE: How long?
OWEN: Nine days.
ANDRE: Nine days in my lot and you never said one word to me.
OWEN: What was I supposed to say?

OWEN: Hers.

OWEN: Hers.

ANDRE: Owen.
OWEN: Don't look at me like that.

OWEN: I handed away a house. I've still got hands.

OWEN: There's the fund. Dad's insurance, plus what I've put in every month since I was twenty-two. Eleven thousand and change.
ANDRE: Go check it. Today, not tomorrow.
OWEN: It's in my name, Andre.
ANDRE: So was your house.
OWEN: It's a bad month. That's all this is.
ANDRE: It's a bad year, brother.
OWEN: Then it's a bad year with a full toolbox.

## SCENE 6: FIRST HERITAGE BANK

MR. HOLT: Mr. Nelson, I've pulled the account.

MR. HOLT: The balance is eleven dollars and forty cents.
OWEN: Say that again.
MR. HOLT: Eleven dollars. Forty cents.
OWEN: There should be eleven thousand in there.

OWEN: I never made a withdrawal.

OWEN: Show me. That's not my hand. Look at the N. I don't loop the N.
MR. HOLT: A teller verified it at the branch counter.
OWEN: Verified against who? Who walked in here?
MR. HOLT: A woman presenting identification as a joint party. Vanessa Nelson.
OWEN: She was never on that account.
MR. HOLT: She was added in March. By signature.
OWEN: By my signature that somebody else wrote.
MR. HOLT: Sir, I'd ask you to keep your voice down in the lobby.
OWEN: My mother's heart valve is failing. That money was the operation.
MR. HOLT: I understand this is difficult.
OWEN: Then put it back.
MR. HOLT: I can't put it back. You file a fraud claim, then a police report, then it goes upstairs to an investigation unit.
OWEN: How long does that take?
MR. HOLT: Ninety days. Often longer.
OWEN: She's got thirty.
MR. HOLT: There's something else in the file. A home equity line drawn against the Bellhaven property.
OWEN: For how much?
MR. HOLT: Sixty thousand. Same signature.
OWEN: So she robbed me twice and this bank held the door for her both times.
MR. HOLT: Mr. Nelson —
OWEN: File the claim. And print me every page with that fake signature on it. Every single one. I'll wait.

## SCENE 7: CROWN LINE REALTY OFFICE

VANESSA: You want it out loud, in front of everybody? Fine. You were a charity project. I was twenty-four and broke and you were kind and steady, and I thought kind and steady was enough. It isn't. I'm done doing charity.

VANESSA: With good arms and no ceiling. That's the whole thing.
