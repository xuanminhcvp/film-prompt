## HOOK

Halvard Reisinger stopped at a rest area on Route 9, lifted the hood of his two-hundred-thousand-dollar car, and worked one connector loose. Nine dollars of copper and plastic.

Twelve miles up the road, Marisol Quintanar was turning her shop sign around.

He rolled in anyway. He talked the whole time she worked, loud enough for the seven people waiting on her benches. He said the last three garages had quoted him four thousand for a transmission. "Name whatever number you want," he said. "I just want the real one first." Then he laid two thousand in cash on her counter, beside a plastic dinosaur wedged on top of her parts cabinet.

Nobody on the benches said a word.

She looked at the money. "I don't quote before I look," she said.

Halvard had run this test nine times that year. He had never once been surprised. That ended in eleven minutes.

To understand the number she wrote on his invoice, go back to the letter folded in her back pocket that morning.
