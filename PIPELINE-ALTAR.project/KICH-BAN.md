## SCENE 1: ST. MICHAEL'S CHURCH — ALTAR

PRIEST: Dear friends, we are gathered here today to join Clara Bennett and Ryan Prescott in holy marriage.
GUEST ONE: The music started twenty-five minutes ago. Where is the groom?
CLARA: He is coming. He is always late. He will be here.
GUEST TWO: Sweetheart, sit down. Your hands are shaking.
CLARA: I am fine. Please, everyone, just give him five more minutes.
RYAN: Stop the music. Stop it. Everyone, be quiet.
CLARA: Ryan? Ryan, why are you holding her hand?
RYAN: Father, give me the microphone. I want all two hundred people in this room to hear this clearly.
PRIEST: Mr. Prescott, this is the house of God. Please lower your voice.
RYAN: I am not marrying her. There. I said it out loud so nobody has to whisper about it later.
CLARA: What are you talking about? Ryan, look at me.
RYAN: I am looking at you. That is the problem. Look at that dress. It is borrowed. The shoes are borrowed. Even the flowers are from the hospital gift shop.
CLARA: You told me you did not care about money.
RYAN: I was twenty-four and stupid. I am thirty-one now, Clara. I want a life. I want a future. What is your future? Night shifts, plastic gloves, and a mother rotting in a hospital bed.
GUEST ONE: My God. Someone stop him.
RYAN: I cannot marry a woman who wipes strangers for a living and has a dying mother attached to her like a debt. Say it with me. I am not marrying a bedpan maid.
CLARA: Her name is Helen. She is my mother. Say her name.
VANESSA: Oh, honey. Nobody in this room wants to know her name.
CLARA: Who are you?
VANESSA: Vanessa Hale. My father owns Hale Group. Ryan works for a company we do business with, and last month he finally learned what a real woman looks like.
CLARA: You knew he was getting married today.
VANESSA: Of course I knew. That is why I came. I wanted to see the dress.
RYAN: Vanessa, do not be cruel.
VANESSA: I am not cruel. I am generous. Here. Compensation for the poor girl.
GUEST TWO: Did she just throw money at her? In a church?
VANESSA: Pick it up. That is more cash than you make in three months, nurse.
CLARA: Eleven thousand. There is eleven thousand dollars here.
VANESSA: See? She counted it. They always count it.
CLARA: My mother's bill is two hundred thousand. Eleven thousand does not even buy her one more week. Keep it. You need it more than I do.
VANESSA: Excuse me?
CLARA: You just paid eleven thousand dollars for a man who left a woman at an altar. That was a very bad price. Keep your money.
RYAN: Do not talk to her like that.
CLARA: I will not talk to her at all. Father, thank you. Everyone, please go home. There is no wedding.
VANESSA: We are done here. Ryan, bring the car around. And somebody move that wheelchair out of the aisle, we cannot get through.
ADRIAN: Sorry.
VANESSA: Was I talking to you? Push him aside, Ryan. I am not stepping over wheels in this dress.
RYAN: Vanessa, just walk around him.
VANESSA: I do not walk around people. That is the entire point of being me.
ADRIAN: Sebastian.
SEBASTIAN: Sir.
ADRIAN: Write down every face in this room. Every single one.
SEBASTIAN: Including the bride, sir?
ADRIAN: Especially her. But put her name on a different list.

## SCENE 2: CHURCH PARKING LOT

CLARA: Hello? Yes, this is Clara Bennett.
BILLING CLERK: Miss Bennett, I am calling about your mother, Helen Bennett, ICU bed twelve. I am very sorry to do this today.
CLARA: Do what today?
BILLING CLERK: The outstanding balance is two hundred and four thousand dollars. The hospital board has issued a final notice. If payment is not received, life support will be withdrawn Thursday at six in the morning.
CLARA: Thursday. That is thirty-one hours.
BILLING CLERK: I am truly sorry. I have three children. I hate this job today.
CLARA: Please. I work there. I am a nurse in that building. I have worked four hundred night shifts in that building.
BILLING CLERK: I know who you are, Miss Bennett. It does not change the number.
CLARA: Mrs. Prescott. Mrs. Prescott, please wait. Please roll down the window.
MRS. PRESCOTT: What do you want? My son has already humiliated himself enough for one morning.
CLARA: Twenty thousand. Not two hundred. Just twenty thousand and I can buy time. I will sign anything. I will work it off for ten years.
MRS. PRESCOTT: Get your hand off my car door.
CLARA: I am begging you. I have never begged anyone in my life. I am on my knees in a parking lot in a wedding dress. Look at me.
MRS. PRESCOTT: I am looking. And do you know what I see? A beggar who almost got into my family.
CLARA: My mother is dying.
MRS. PRESCOTT: Then let her die like poor people do. Quietly. A beggar does not get to touch the Prescott name.
CLARA: My hand. My hand is in the door.
MRS. PRESCOTT: Then move it.
