## HOOK

"Mr. Kittredge, don't let them back on that ice. Please."

Ivorine Baptiste said it with a mop handle in one hand and a folded paper towel in the other. The manager of Hollis Creek Ice Center laughed. Then he said it again, louder, so the boards would carry it. "She reads gauges now."

Four hundred and twelve people were in the building that night. The laugh went up the bleachers and came back down. A referee checked his watch. Nobody stood up.

Ivorine did not raise her voice.

"The frost on that line stops halfway," she said. "It has never stopped there before."

Dale Kittredge had her walked out the loading door at nine forty. He had run this rink eleven years. He did not know what Ivorine had spent nineteen years doing before she ever picked up a mop.

The next public skate was at eight the following morning. Ten hours.

She had clocked in at six that evening, the way she had every night for four years.
