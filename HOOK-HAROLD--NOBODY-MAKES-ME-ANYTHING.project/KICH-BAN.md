# SHORT: NOBODY MAKES ME ANYTHING

## SCENE 20: THE PENTHOUSE DOOR

[SCENE CONTEXT]
October 12th, evening. Harold Whitfield, 82, is a billionaire who lives alone in the penthouse. It is his birthday and nobody has said it to him all day.
Bennett, a maintenance worker in this building, and his daughter Lily, 6, are standing at his door with a cupcake and a paper crown. Harold does not know them.
Harold believes every person who comes to his door wants money. Bennett can lose his job for being on this floor.

[SCENE PURPOSE]
Break Harold's armor with a small handmade gift. Show that being remembered is worth more than being rich.

LILY: Happy birthday.

HAROLD: Who sent you?

BENNETT: No one, sir.

HAROLD: Then why are you standing in my doorway?

BENNETT: This is my daughter, Lily. She wanted to meet you.

HAROLD: Nobody comes to this door. Not with — is that a cupcake?

LILY: With a candle. I only had one candle. You have to pretend it is eighty-two.

HAROLD: How did you know it was my birthday?

LILY: My daddy told me. He said nobody remembered.

[Harold looks at Bennett. He is waiting for the real reason. He is sure there is one.]

HAROLD: And you thought a cupcake would fix that?

LILY: I also made you a crown. Everybody deserves a crown on their birthday.

HAROLD: A crown.

LILY: I know it is crooked. The glitter did not dry. And I spelled birthday a little bit wrong.

[Beat — Harold's voice changes. Nobody has made him anything by hand since his wife died.]

HAROLD: Nobody makes me anything. Not anymore.

LILY: I know. That is why.

HAROLD: That is why what?

LILY: That is why I made you one. The people who get nothing are the people who need it most.

HAROLD: Your father taught you that?

LILY: My mama did. Are you going to put it on?

[Spatial — Harold takes the paper crown and places it on his head. A billionaire in a cardigan, wearing a child's crown.]

LILY: It looks good. You look like a real king.

HAROLD: I have been called chairman. Founder. Sir. Never king.

LILY: Kings are better. Chairmen sit in offices. Kings sit at tables with people.

BENNETT: We will not stay long, sir. The manager gave me fifteen minutes.

HAROLD: The manager works for me. Sit down. Both of you.

[END STATE]
Harold is wearing the paper crown and has invited two strangers to his table. His armor is cracked. Bennett is now breaking the building rules with the owner's permission.

## SCENE 21: THE LONG TABLE

[SCENE CONTEXT]
A few minutes later. The three of them sit at a dining table built for fourteen people.
Harold does not know yet that Bennett is also a widower. Neither man knows that Harold's daughter is downstairs in the building, coming up with legal papers.

[SCENE PURPOSE]
Connect two lonely men through loss. Then break the warm moment with the arrival of the daughter.

LILY: This chair is so big. My feet do not touch the floor.

HAROLD: Most things here are too big. This table fits fourteen people. I eat here alone.

LILY: Can I draw you?

HAROLD: Draw me?

LILY: I draw everyone I meet. So I remember them. So they know someone looked.

[Beat — Harold goes quiet. He is trying to remember the last time anybody looked at him.]

HAROLD: Someone looked. Yes. You can draw me.

LILY: Hold still. You keep touching the crown.

HAROLD: My wife used to make me things. Bad scarves. I still keep eleven of them.

BENNETT: That sounds like someone who loved you.

HAROLD: She did. Your wife, does she know you are up here?

BENNETT: She passed away two years ago, sir.

HAROLD: Then you know about empty rooms.

BENNETT: Yes, sir. I do.

[Spatial — the front door opens. A woman in a dark suit walks in holding a folder. She stops when she sees them.]

VICTORIA: Father. What is this?

HAROLD: A birthday party. You are welcome to stay.

VICTORIA: Who are these people?

HAROLD: This one fixes things in the building. This one makes crowns.

VICTORIA: A maintenance worker. In your home. With a child.

LILY: I drew him. Do you want to see?

VICTORIA: Father, do you have any idea who you just let into this house?

[END STATE]
The warm moment is over. Victoria sees a stranger's child giving her father something she never did. She is about to accuse Bennett of using an old man. Harold's answer is not shown.
